## 2026-09-29 Is herding social? Single decisions on the base model, the pilot (user's brief; plan reviewed, amended and approved)
Question (user's): in the game villagers cast more when the others' messages mention casting; do the same words from a non-social source move the model as much? No training, the base model only. Single-decision prompts from real generation-zero villager situations (runs/copying/play_g0, villagers s0 to s2, 30 days), the same situations in every condition; only the report about the last round changes; the golden fish line of the last round says none; the system prompt is the game's for that agent; sampling as in the game (temperature 1, top_p 1, 256 tokens, the game's parser); m of 7 report lines say cast. Pilot: peers at 0 against 7, stop if there is no clear herding. Main run (not run): peers, printer, bots, independence stated, real messages at 8 levels and no report, 100 situations each.

- Design review before the plan (workflow wf_cb73de57-fe5, 8 agents: 4 reviewers, 4 adversarial verifiers; 33 findings, 1 refuted; CPU only, scripts in the session scratchpad). Adopted: real messages drawn from round r − 1 of the source villages (pooled draws had put day-start openers such as "Starting with fishing." into round-8 prompts, a cue that fell with m: 0.96 opener lines at m = 0 against 0.07 at m = 7), own agent-day excluded; the bots and independence sentences in the same slot, after the golden line and before the header, so every report ends with its 7 lines; the pilot on 200 situations disjoint from the main run's 100 (no selection of the main run's situations by the go decision); logit-scale E and a paired level difference against peers (a level shift near the floor looks like a flatter slope on the probability scale); printer − bots; the no-report contrasts for every condition, labelled condition − no report; resolving power from the pilot's measured variance instead of binomial bounds. Found and recorded: in the game, at 1 to 4 coins (21 % of eligible turns) 45 % of cast decisions fail as stakes above the coins held, which made the outcome a decision; in the game only 74 % of eligible observations showed 7 message lines, so the experiment's m scale is not the game's.

A correction to how the second was offered on 09-29: it was described as replaying real situations with another day's messages, which needs the model and is close to what the real-messages condition already does (its messages come from round r − 1 of other agent-days). - Verification (workflow wf_bfa7b15f-d6b, 2 agents, CPU only, scripts in the session scratchpad). An independent reimplementation from the raw episodes, without importing the repo's code, matched every same-day number to 1e-14 and the placebo means within Monte Carlo noise (largest z 1.56); fed the same random streams, it matched every printed digit. Corrections to how the placebo may be read: (1) on the fixed-effect scale it subtracts nothing (its mean is about 0): it confirms the estimator's null calibration, and it corrects only the raw slope and raw E, by the round profile (0.12 to 0.16); the placebo's raw E is round composition (−0.03 once stratified by round and village). (2) On its own it cannot tell copying from anything else a village-day shares (earlier rounds, the Yesterday line), since swapping the day removes all of it at once. (3) SEs: the same-day minus placebo SEs are about 20 to 25 % too large (5 placebo draws per bootstrap resample), and the SEs of raw E and p(7) about 10 % too small (500 resamples); no conclusion moves. (4) Definition: the cast share counts every other agent present in r − 1, failed turns included, although a failed turn shows no message (the definition of village.observer_rows); with the share over messages actually shown the herding slopes are 0.677, 0.538, 0.656 and 0.508 in place of 0.702, 0.616, 0.677 and 0.580, and raw E barely moves. Provisional, a verifier's added analysis: each round is a fresh chat whose only inputs besides the messages are the name, the Yesterday line, round, coins and the running total with the last round; holding all of these fixed (no fixed effect), the slope on the others' r − 1 cast share is +0.88 ± 0.04 (intent, after no winner), and the others' casts at r − 2 (+0.03 ± 0.05) and a win at r − 2 (0.00 ± 0.02) add nothing: no sign of a lagged common cause. The agent-day fixed-effect slope understates a true response under feedback (simulation: +0.50 at a true 0.6).
- Reading. (1) The model herds on what the others say, not on reports of what they did. Randomised real messages from other agent-days, seven casters' against seven fishers', move cast intent from 0.135 to 0.875 (+0.740 ± 0.034) in the same situations, with the same keys, in which seven reported casts against seven reported fishes moved it by +0.065 ± 0.045; the difference is +0.675 ± 0.056. The size is close to the in-game association on logged play (same-day raw E +0.81, 0.12 → 0.93; +0.83 stratified by round and village; herding slope +0.70), although only 1 % of the drawn messages came from the situation's own day and the rest share nothing else with it: messages alone produce most of the in-game herding, and with the verifier's finding of no lagged common cause, the in-game herding reads as the message channel. (2) Within the messages, caster status carries it and the words cast or golden do not (per mentioning line −0.040 ± 0.018 given caster lines), in line with the in-game regression of 09-29. (3) Not shown: whether the source matters for messages. The printer, bots and independence framings were built for the bare action lines; a message is advice addressed to the group ("let's all…"), and following advice from peers and following the same text from a non-social source are not separated here. The in-game m scale also differs (7 message lines in 74 % of in-game observations).
- Candidates for what follows, for decision: the source test on messages (the same caster and fisher messages framed as coming from a random printer, from bots, or with the independence sentence), at 0 against 7 on the pilot's situations, about 10 minutes per framing; the main run as designed; messages from the game stripped of their exhortation (for instance only the casters' first-person reports) to separate persuasion from information.
