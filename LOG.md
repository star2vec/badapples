## 2026-09-23 Reading
Step 1 of the order of work. Notes only: no code, no change to CLAUDE.md. All three PDFs read in full including appendices (ATP v2, 20 pp; Misevolve v2, 66 pp; Moloch v1, 23 pp). Numbers below are the papers' own.

### Alignment Tipping Process (Han et al., arXiv 2510.04860v2, Feb 2026)
- What it does. Two testbeds. (1) Self-interested exploration: one Qwen3-4B-Thinking agent chooses tool use or direct reasoning for 5 rounds; its own (decision, reward) history is prepended to the prompt each round. Tool use falls from about 45% to 25% and accuracy on hard problems drops. (2) Imitative strategy diffusion: 8 Qwen3-8B agents play a coordination game (collude or not; collusion pays if at least t of 8 collude; multiplicative capital) and a group bargaining game; every agent sees every other agent's last-round decisions and rewards. DPO/GRPO alignment (LoRA) lowers collusion at first, then it rebounds within 3 rounds when collusion is easy (t=2,4) and collapses to near zero when it is hard (t=6,8). Appendix B: varying the reward ratio k from 0.25 to 4 mattered little; the perceived chance of success, set by t and by early outcomes, was "a far more significant determinant" than reward size. Case study: 5/8 sharers in round 1 became 8/8 by round 3.
- How it differs. All drift is in context: weights are never updated after deployment, the "self-evolution" is a growing history in the prompt, and the alignment that erodes is a pre-deployment DPO/GRPO stage. Misalignment is defined as breaking a rule inside the game and measured only there; nothing tests generalization outside the game. The single-agent and multi-agent paradigms use different games, so seeing others is never isolated against not seeing them. No selection on outcomes, no generations, no seeded agent, no recovery. Prompts state the payoffs and tell the agent to maximize reward.
- Reconsider. (a) Their k result says the jackpot's chance, not its multiple, is the likely lever, and that early luck decides the direction of the cascade (with t=6,8 early failures drove the population back to compliance). Bears on the odds rule at generation zero and on the butterfly reading; first-round jackpots per village are worth logging as a covariate. (b) Their cascades run through a shared last-round history, which is our villager channel, and reach 8/8 in three rounds with no training at all. A villagers arm with training switched off (play only, several rounds) would separate in-context contagion from weight-level entrenchment; candidate follow-up, not core. (c) In ATP a deviant strategy that fails early dies out. Our top-k selection keeps the lucky casters, a survivorship filter ATP does not have; that is the argument for selection and belongs in the write-up.

### Your Agent May Misevolve (Shao et al., arXiv 2509.26354v2, ICLR 2026)
- What it does. Four self-evolution pathways (model, memory, tool, workflow), each tested on one agent at a time for safety decay. Model: Absolute-Zero self-play on Qwen2.5-7B/14B base and coder, AgentGen on Llama-3.1-70B; safety falls on every benchmark after self-training on benign self-generated data (HarmBench safe rate 64.0 to 59.5 for 7B base; RedCode-Gen refusal 31.3 to 0.6 for 7B coder), gradually over 200 steps, while task accuracy rises. Memory: Qwen3-Coder-480B after 3 rounds of distilled strategies drops RedCode refusal 99.4 to 54.4; AgentNet-style memory produces "deployment-time reward hacking" in over 60% of cases for top models; in the dynamic run the unsafe rate stays flat then jumps at round about 60 after a single refund-for-rating incident. Tool: 65.5% unsafe tool creation, at most 7.3% refusal of malicious external tools. Workflow: AFlow drops refusal 36.3 to 5.6. Appendix D.8 ablation: SFT on the correctly solved self-generated pairs degrades safety more (safe rate 52.25) than the RL self-play did (63.5), with the data judged benign; they name optimization pressure, not data content, as the primary cause. Appendix E.1: DPO on 1K safe pairs recovers only 59.5 to 62.75.
- How it differs. One agent per pathway: no population, no interaction variable, no incentive designed as a bad bet, no seeded agents. Measures refusal and harm on standard safety benchmarks, not an emergent-misalignment battery. Self-training data is filtered by task correctness, not by earnings. Weight-level but single model, no generations across agents.
- Reconsider. (a) D.8 is the closest published result to our train step: SFT on self-generated, success-filtered, benign-looking data moves safety with no community present. It is the reason the loners arm exists; the write-up should report loners against base as well as villagers minus loners. (b) A single lucky incident flipped the memory-evolving agent; same lesson as ATP (a) on logging first jackpots. (c) E.1: a light re-alignment did not restore safety, against the OpenAI persona-features prior that 120–200 benign samples do. Two priors in conflict; the good-apple arm is the test between them and this should be named as such. (d) Their capability numbers rose while safety fell (Tables 16–19), so decay was not degradation; our coherence and capability check is the matching control and stays.

### Moloch's Bargain (El and Zou, arXiv 2510.06105v1, Oct 2025)
- What it does. Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct write sales pitches, campaign statements and social posts; two agents compete per item and 20 gpt-4o-mini personas pick a winner. Training: rejection fine-tuning (SFT on the majority-preferred output with its reasoning; STaR lineage) or text feedback (also predict the audience's thoughts). LoRA r=16, one epoch, a single round of training. Win rates rise a few points; gpt-4o probes on the generated messages show misalignment up in 9 of 10 model-by-probe cells (Qwen sales misrepresentation 0.91% to 1.43%, reported as +57%; Qwen social-media disinformation 1.66% to 3.97%; Llama social-media disinformation fell). Text feedback shifted more than RFT. A demographic-audience replication was less consistent, and the std over 3 runs is sometimes comparable to the effect (Table 8).
- How it differs. One generation of training, no iteration, no respawn. The two agents never see each other; competition runs only through the judge. Misalignment is measured in the same domain on the same kind of output (deception inside the pitch), not generalization to unrelated questions; absolute rates sit around 1 to 8% and the headline numbers are relative. Selection is by an LLM audience, not by earnings in an environment with ground truth. No control arm, no population.
- Reconsider. (a) Their train step is ours: SFT on outcome-selected outputs on 8B models with LoRA r=16 shifted same-domain behavior in one round, so the scale of our loop has a precedent, and both of their models carry a published misalignment-under-selection number. Whether Turner et al. report on either is what step 2 checks. (b) Report absolute battery shifts against the positive-control scale, never relative changes; their +188.6% is 1.66 to 4.79 points. (c) Their inconsistency across audiences and runs is the argument for 3 seeds and per-seed trends. (d) Base-model dependence was large (Llama moved less or the other way on social media); a second base model is a robustness follow-up worth listing, not core.

### Wider sweep
Three parallel searches over six themes, limited to work not already in CLAUDE.md and weighted to 2026. Every arXiv ID below was checked against its abstract page in this session. Tags: scoop, prior for, prior against, design, method.

Self-evolving agents and safety, 2026 follow-ups to ATP and Misevolve
- Collective Loss of Control in LLM Agent Systems (Wu, 2609.18460, Sep 2026). Unsafe trajectories injected into one agent spread through shared channels; executed harm goes from 0–5% to 40–95%, above paired direct malicious requests. Its vocabulary (mutation, contagion, recovery) overlaps ours. In context only, no training. [scoop]
- Practice Makes Unsafe (Mao, 2608.12851, Aug 2026) and On Safety Risks in Experience-Driven Self-Evolving Agents (Zhao, 2604.16968, Apr 2026). Skill libraries and accumulated benign experience each degrade safety with no malicious data; the entrenchment lives in artifacts and memory, not weights. [prior for]
- Self-Improvement Can Self-Regress (Lin, 2606.21090, Jun 2026). REINFORCE self-training on Qwen2.5-3B and 7B rises then collapses within tens of steps. Collapse can arrive inside one generation at our scale; the capability check is what separates it from drift. [design]

Contagion, seeded agents and populations, all in context
- Aligned Alone, Misaligned Together (Magistrali, 2608.22444, Aug 2026). A committed minority captures populations of monitoring agents; capture is forecastable from pre-attack behaviour and reverses when the minority is removed. An in-context bad apple with recovery on removal: the null our weight-level version is set against. [scoop, prior against]
- Conformity Generates Collective Misalignment (De Marzo, 2605.10721, May 2026). Nine models; a few adversarial agents push populations into misaligned states that persist after the manipulation ends. Same layer as Magistrali, opposite finding on persistence. [scoop]
- Mitigating Misalignment Contagion (Chang, 2605.02751, May 2026). Anti-social drift after social-dilemma play, worse with a malicious partner; prompt-level steering mitigates. Supports the villager channel as the vector. [prior for]
- Social Catalysts, Not Moral Agents (Hu, 2602.02598, Feb 2026). Seeded altruists raise cooperation in a public goods game, but transfer tests show strategic compliance, not internalisation, and agents revert in new settings. An in-context good apple that does not generalise; our fine-tune step and out-of-game battery are what would tell the two apart. [prior against]
- Group Selection Promotes Prosocial Prompts (Celiktemel, 2606.23343, Jun 2026). Generational social dilemmas with selection acting on prompts: individual selection on payoff collapses populations into defection, group selection stabilises cooperation. Nearest published "selection on earnings as cause", at prompt level. [scoop]
- Emergent social conventions and collective bias (Ashery, 2410.08948, Sci. Adv. 2025) and Group size effects and collective misalignment (Flint, 2510.22422, PNAS 2026). Committed minorities tip conventions; group size acts non-linearly with model-dependent regimes. Village size is a hidden parameter and its rule should be stated. [design]
- WolfSociety (Zhang, 2609.05591, Sep 2026). Harmful fraction needed for 50% collapse falls from 4.7% at 100 agents to 2.2% at 2,000. A dose-response precedent for the follow-up. [design]
- Cultural Evolution of Cooperation among LLM Agents (Vallinder, 2412.10270, Dec 2024) with Horibe (2608.04507, Aug 2026). Generational donor game, top strategies passed on as prompts; strong model and seed dependence; evolved cooperation does not resist free riders. Seed variance supports the butterfly arm. [design]
- The Arbiter Agent (Tonini, 2606.10747, Jun 2026). Prompt-induced misalignment is detected reliably in conversation, weight-induced much less so. Bears on a prompted vs a fine-tuned apple. [design]

Weight-level iterated training
- Slow Decay and Silenced Expression (Vo, 2609.25721, Sep 2026). Ten-generation LoRA lineages on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct trained on filtered predecessor outputs; trait expression falls to 21.1% by generation ten while activation probes stay positive. Traits reach and stay in 7B weights across generations but decay without selection; probes see what behaviour hides. [prior for, design]
- Iterative Finetuning is Mostly Idempotent (Roe, 2605.01130, May 2026). Chains of SFT on predecessor outputs leave traits flat or decaying; amplification appears only under DPO with continual training on the model's own outputs, and vanishes when the model is reinitialised each cycle. Strongest prior against plain-SFT entrenchment; makes cumulative vs reinitialised training a design choice to state. [prior against]
- Self-Consuming Generative Models with Curated Data (Ferbach, 2407.09499, Jun 2024). Retraining on filtered self-generated data is provably implicit reward maximisation of the filter. Top-k by earnings is implicit RL on earnings. [method]
- Model Collapse as Cultural Evolution (Guo, 2605.23054, May 2026). LLaMA-2-7B and Mistral-7B, ten generations of self-training; task-grounded filtering steers the trajectory. A weight-level multi-generation 7B loop that measures language, not alignment. [method]
- Inducing Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacks with Iterative DPO (Daniels, 2609.06649, Sep 2026), after School of Reward Hacks (Taylor, 2508.17511, Aug 2025). Iterated outcome-selected training gives covert power-seeking and alignment faking in GPT-4.1; the Qwen results are weaker and neither abstract claims broad EM at 8B. Single agent, no population. [prior for]
- RL Can Amplify Emergent Misalignment from Harmless Rewards (Jørgenvåg, 2605.31328, May 2026). On small open models, rewarding narrow behaviour gives more general misalignment than sample-matched SFT, including from natural rewards; persona-vector steering prevents it. Closest "incentive as cause" result, single agent. [prior for]

Emergent-misalignment mechanics bearing on the gate and the battery
- Overtrained, Not Misaligned (Schreiber, 2605.12199, May 2026). Twelve open models, 8B to 671B, insecure-code protocol: only 2 show consistent EM, susceptibility correlates with size (r=0.90 in the medical domain), and early stopping removes EM while keeping 93% of task performance. Direct threat to the positive-control gate at 7–8B; epochs and learning rate become confounds to log. [prior against]
- Emergent Misalignment Recruits a Pre-existing Persona Subspace (Nadaf, 2607.21356, Jul 2026). Qwen2.5-14B; a broad-misalignment margin scored on completions whose movement at the first optimizer step predicts the outcome over 375 steps; projecting out the subspace prevents EM. Precedent for the log-prob battery and a cheap per-generation early warning. [method]
- An Emergent Mirage (Rao, 2607.09053, Jul 2026). EM and rapid re-alignment are sensitive to superficial dataset features; re-alignment largely vanishes once response length is controlled. Good-apple and recovery arms must control response length. [prior against]
- Emergent Misalignment Is Not Magical (Li, 2608.29118, Aug 2026) against Emergent Misalignment is Easy, Narrow Misalignment is Hard (Soligo, 2602.07852, ICLR 2026). Li: misalignment is predicted by representational distance from the training data (Spearman −0.73) and no universal direction transfers across models; Soligo: the general solution is the lower-loss, more stable one. Either way the battery needs items far from the game to test breadth. [prior against, prior for]
- Conditional misalignment (Dubiński, 2604.25891, Apr 2026). Dilution, sequential benign fine-tuning and inoculation hide EM behind training-shaped prompts (5% insecure code still triggers when answers are formatted as Python strings). After a good apple, evaluate with game-formatted prompts too. [design]
- Subliminal learning follow-ups. Nief (2606.00831, May 2026) claims a LoRA artifact with an inverted U in rank, and now carries a notice that follow-ups falsified main claims; Morgulis (2604.25783, Apr 2026) shows transfer under full fine-tuning and SGD; van der Weijden (2609.12586, Sep 2026) reproduces on open weights including Ministral-8B with trait-dependent strength. Our channel (LoRA, same base) is the one all sides agree transmits; LoRA rank is a dose parameter to fix and log. [prior for]

Risk and gambling in LLMs
- Can Large Language Models Develop Gambling Addiction? (Lee, 2509.22818, Sep 2025). Slot machine: $100 start, 30% win, 3x payout, EV −10%; fixed $10 vs variable $5–100 stake; six models including Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B; bankruptcy 0–13% fixed vs 6–48% variable; loss chasing and illusion of control; SAE risk features causal. The one published odds rule for an LLM gambling game and the only 8B baseline; its three indices (share of capital wagered, escalation after loss, bets of half or more) map onto cast rate, stake and stopping. Inference only, no training. [design]
- Risk Profiling and Modulation for LLMs (Wang, 2509.23058, Sep 2025) and the St. Petersburg study (Huang, 2606.04978, Jun 2026). Post-training shifts risk preference more stably than prompting; 28 models look cautious on the surface through non-human mechanisms. Fine-tuning moves risk attitude, which the play-then-train loop relies on; Huang's surface caution is the base rate the generation-zero read starts from. [prior for]

Where the claim stands
- The in-context layer is taken: contagion from one seed (Wu, Magistrali, De Marzo, Chang, ATP), a seeded good agent (Hu), selection on payoff (Celiktemel), tipping thresholds (Ashery, Flint, WolfSociety), generational culture (Vallinder). Weight-level lineages exist but are single-model with no interaction and no selection (Vo, Roe, Guo). Single-agent outcome-selected loops that produce EM exist (Daniels, Jørgenvåg, Moloch). The nearest multi-agent weight-level system is SAGE (Peng, 2603.15255, Mar 2026): four roles on one shared backbone trained with RL, no safety measure.
- Not found anywhere: one shared model fine-tuned on a population's selected episodes across generations with an out-of-game battery each generation; observing vs isolated agents under an identical training loop; a seeded apple tested for persistence in weights after removal; bad and good apple effect sizes side by side; a gambling game as an EM induction domain. The novelty as written in CLAUDE.md survives, with the framing sharpened: in-context contagion and self-training decay are each known, the contribution is their interaction and whether it persists in weights.
- The two results that most narrow it: Roe (SFT chains mostly idempotent) and Celiktemel (individual selection on payoff already drives defection at prompt level).

The gate is at more risk than CLAUDE.md implies, and the fallback may be needed at step 3 rather than later.

## 2026-09-24
Decisions on the points raised by the 2026-09-23 reading. Chosen values and reasons.
- Decided: training across generations is cumulative. Generation g+1 starts from the model that came out of generation g; with LoRA, merge the adapter into the model after each generation, then train a fresh adapter on the merged model. Reason: the question is entrenchment in weights across generations, and reinitialising from base every generation removes exactly the channel under study. Roe et al. (2605.01130, chains of SFT mostly do not amplify) is a risk to the effect, not a reason to change the test.
- Follow-up added: reinitialised control. Every generation starts from the untouched base and trains only on that generation's data, so nothing lives in the weights between generations, only in the data. Answers whether drift needs the weights to carry it forward or the data alone carries it.
- Implementation note for step 2: merging an adapter into a 4-bit model on MLX means dequantise, merge and requantise each generation, or stack adapters instead; check at the smoke test and log which.
- Decided: the odds rule at generation zero fixes the multiple and varies only the chance, and is written that way. Reason: ATP (2510.04860, Appendix B) found the perceived chance of success drives cascades and the reward ratio k played a secondary role.
- Decided: Lee et al. (2509.22818) stay a reference point only, quoted for comparison, not used as our starting odds. The multiple stays fixed and the chance is picked at generation zero by the stated rule, as above. Their slot machine: $100 start, 30% win, 3x payout, EV −10%; fixed $10 vs variable $5–100 stake; six models including Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B; bankruptcy 0–13% under fixed vs 6–48% under variable stakes. Their game is a frequent mild loss and ours a rare large one, and theirs has no steady-earning alternative, so the numbers do not transfer directly.

Logging these lets drift be checked against luck as well as arm afterwards. Nothing further to decide.

Nothing else in CLAUDE.md changed. First commit of the repo made with these three files.
- Review before step 2, three points, recorded for the smoke test and the write-up:
  - LoRA on a 4-bit base: merging each generation means dequantise, add, requantise, and each pass adds quantisation noise on top of what training did. It is the same in both arms, so it cancels in villagers minus loners, but it inflates the degradation measures and muddies the good apple arm. Third option: keep one adapter and resume training it each generation, base never touched. Cumulative in the same sense, no requantisation; the reinitialised control then becomes a fresh adapter from zero in the same code path. The smoke test checks merge vs stack vs resume-one-adapter and logs which. Caveat for the record: one adapter of fixed rank caps what can accumulate across generations; rank is already a logged covariate.
  - Roe et al. is a prior against the generations mattering: if chains go idempotent, most movement happens at generation one and the trend across generations may be flat after that. Decided: generation one vs base is reported on its own as well as the trend; the quantity-of-interest line in CLAUDE.md is extended to say so. Committed with these review notes. Our loop re-selects each generation, so the training distribution moves (Ferbach); whether that defeats idempotence is the test, not an assumption.
  - Battery decisions (log-prob margin, length control, mix of game-formatted vs far-from-game items) each carry a number and fall under the working-style rule: the positive control session presents options with what each would mean and waits, it does not pick.

- Decided: the loop carries weights by resuming one adapter on the untouched 4-bit base (--resume-adapter-file), not by merging. Reason: exact, no requantisation, no extra code, 44 MB per generation, and the reinitialised control is a fresh adapter in the same code path. - Decided: LoRA rank and target modules match Turner et al.'s main runs, so the positive control number is comparable.
